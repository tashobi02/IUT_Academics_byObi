Fundamental Theorems of Calculus
Practice Assignment · Department of Mathematics

1   The Derivative

The derivative of $f(x)$ with respect to $x$ is defined as the following limit:

$$f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} \tag{1}$$

For any $n \in \mathbb{R}$, the power rule states:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(x^n\right) = nx^{n-1} \tag{2}$$

2   Integration

The power rule for indefinite integration is given in Equation 3:

$$\int x^n\,dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C, \qquad n \neq -1 \tag{3}$$

The famous Gaussian integral evaluates to:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\,dx = \sqrt{\pi} \tag{4}$$

3   Rotation Matrix

A rotation in $\mathbb{R}^2$ by angle $\theta$ is represented by the matrix:

$$R(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}$$

Its determinant satisfies:

$$\begin{vmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{vmatrix} = \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1 \tag{5}$$

4   Basic Trigonometric Identity

The following identities follow from the Pythagorean theorem:

$$\begin{gather*} \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1 \\[6pt] \tan^2\theta + 1 = \sec^2\theta \\[6pt] 1 + \cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta \end{gather*}$$

Linear Systems and Vector Spaces
Practice Assignment · Department of Applied Mathematics

1   System of Linear Equations

Consider the following system of three equations in three unknowns:

$$\begin{align} 2x + 3y - z &= 7 \\ x - y + 2z &= -3 \\ 3x + y + z &= 10 \end{align}$$

This is written compactly in matrix form $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ as:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 \\ 3 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ -3 \\ 10 \end{pmatrix}$$

2   Vector Operations

2.1   Dot Product

The dot product of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is:

$$\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} u_i v_i = |\vec{u}||\vec{v}|\cos\theta \tag{4}$$

2.2   The $L^2$ Norm

The Euclidean norm of a vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} v_i^2} \tag{5}$$

3   Eigenvalue Problem

For a matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, the eigenvalue equation is:

$$A\mathbf{x} = \lambda\mathbf{x}, \qquad \lambda \in \mathbb{C} \tag{6}$$

The eigenvalues are roots of the characteristic polynomial:

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = 0 \tag{7}$$

4   Taylor Series Expansion

The Taylor expansion of a smooth function $f(x)$ around the point $x = a$:

$$\begin{equation} f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}\,(x - a)^n \end{equation}$$

For $a = 0$ and $f(x) = e^x$, this reduces to:

$$e^x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!} = 1 + x + \frac{x^2}{2!} + \frac{x^3}{3!} + \cdots$$

Gradient Descent and Optimization
Practice Assignment · Department of Computer Science & Engineering

1   Loss Function

Given a training set $\{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ with predictions $\hat{y}_i = \theta^T x_i$, the mean squared error loss is:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(y_i - \hat{y}_i\right)^2 \tag{1}$$

where $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the parameter vector and $\hat{y}_i = \theta^T x_i$.

2   Gradient Computation

The gradient of $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to $\theta$ is derived step by step:

$$\begin{align} \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\theta} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(y_i - \theta^T x_i\right)^2\right] \\[6pt] &= \frac{-2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(y_i - \theta^T x_i\right)x_i \\[6pt] &= \frac{-2}{N}X^T(y - X\theta) \end{align}$$

3   Normal Equation

Setting the gradient in Equation (2) to zero and solving for $\hat{\theta}$ yields the closed-form solution:

$$\hat{\theta} = \left(X^TX\right)^{-1}X^Ty \tag{5}$$

where $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ is the design matrix and $y \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is the target vector.

4   Gradient Descent Update Rule

Starting from an initial $\theta^{(0)}$, the parameter is updated at each iteration $t$:

$$\theta^{(t+1)} = \theta^{(t)} - \alpha \cdot \nabla_\theta\,\mathcal{L}\!\left(\theta^{(t)}\right) \tag{6}$$

Substituting the gradient from Section 2, this expands to:

$$\begin{equation} \begin{split} \theta^{(t+1)} &= \theta^{(t)} - \alpha \cdot \frac{-2}{N}X^T\!\left(y - X\theta^{(t)}\right) \\[4pt] &= \theta^{(t)} + \frac{2\alpha}{N}\,X^T\!\left(y - X\theta^{(t)}\right) \end{split} \end{equation}$$

5   Convergence Criterion

Gradient descent is said to have converged when the gradient magnitude falls below a tolerance:

$$\left\|\nabla_\theta\,\mathcal{L}\!\left(\theta^{(t)}\right)\right\|_2 \leq \varepsilon \tag{8}$$

for some pre-specified $\varepsilon > 0$. The softmax function used for multiclass output is:

$$\sigma(\mathbf{z})_j = \frac{e^{z_j}}{\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K} e^{z_k}}, \qquad j = 1, 2, \ldots, K \tag{9}$$
